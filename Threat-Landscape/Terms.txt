Q:- What is PSIRT?
ANS : PSIRT stands for Product Security Incident Response Team.
    It is a specialized team within an organization that is responsible 
    for managing and responding to security incidents related to the organization's 
    products. The PSIRT works to identify, analyze, and mitigate vulnerabilities and 
    threats to ensure the security and integrity of the products offered by the
    organization. They also communicate with customers and stakeholders about security 
    issues and provide guidance on best practices for product security.
    
    Maturity Levels:
        The capabilities of a PSIRT can vary depending on its maturity level, ranging from
        basic to more advanced capabilities.
        

SIRT (Security Incident Response Team) - Also commonly referred to as CSIRT (Computer Security Incident Response Team) or sometimes simply IRT (Incident Response Team).

A SIRT/CSIRT is responsible for managing and responding to any security incident that 
affects an organization's internal IT infrastructure, networks, systems, and data.


A PSIRT is a specialized team that focuses exclusively on security vulnerabilities and 
incidents related to the products or services that an organization develops, sells, or offers to its customers.



Q:- What is a Patch Tuesday?
ANS : Patch Tuesday is a term used to refer to the second Tuesday of each month when Microsoft releases
      security updates and patches for its software products. This practice was established by Microsoft 
      to provide a predictable schedule for IT administrators and users to apply critical security updates 
      to their systems. 
      Patch Tuesday typically includes updates for Windows operating systems, Microsoft Office, and other 
      Microsoft software products. The goal is to help organizations maintain the security and stability 
      of their systems by addressing known vulnerabilities in a timely manner.
 
    Realtionship between the psirt and patch Tuesday:

    A PSIRT is the team within a company (e.g., Microsoft's PSIRT, Adobe's PSIRT) that identifies, analyzes, 
    and develops fixes for security vulnerabilities in their products.
    Patch Tuesday is the scheduled day when many of these fixes, developed by the PSIRT and engineering teams,
     are publicly released to customers.


Q:- What are advisories?
Ans:- In the context of cybersecurity, advisories are formal documents or notifications that provide detailed 
        information about security vulnerabilities, threats, or incidents. Their main purpose is to inform individuals, 
        organizations, and the broader cybersecurity community about risks and provide guidance on how to mitigate them
Who issues advisories?
    Advisories are issued by various entities, including:

    Software and Hardware Vendors:
                                    Companies like Microsoft, Apple, Cisco, Adobe, Oracle, etc., release advisories for vulnerabilities found 
                                in their own products. These are often issued by their Product Security Incident Response Teams (PSIRTs).
    Government Cybersecurity Agencies: 
                                    Organizations like CISA (Cybersecurity and Infrastructure Security Agency) in the U.S
    ,                                ACSC (Australian Cyber Security Centre), NCSC (National Cyber Security Centre) in the UK,
    Security Research Organizations:
    CERTs (Computer Emergency Response Teams): 
                    National or sectoral CERTs (like US-CERT, JPCERT/CC) often aggregate and disseminate advisories from various sources to their constituents.



Q:- What is the advisories feed?
Ans:- An advisories feed (often referred to as a security advisories feed or vulnerability intelligence feed) is a continuous, 
      automated stream of information about newly discovered or updated cybersecurity vulnerabilities, threats, and sometimes related incidents.

        Think of it like a news ticker for security flaws. Instead of manually checking individual vendor websites or government security portals 
        for new advisories, an advisories feed aggregates this information and delivers it in a structured, often machine-readable, format.

        How Advisories Feeds Work:

            Sources: Advisories feeds pull data from a wide variety of sources, including:

                Vendor PSIRTs: Product Security Incident Response Teams from software and hardware companies 
                            (e.g., Microsoft, Cisco, Adobe, Apple) publish advisories about vulnerabilities in their own products.
